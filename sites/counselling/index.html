How I can help

I can help you make sense of what's happening and find a way forward that works for you.

I am an experienced counsellor who first qualified in 1996. I offer a comfortable, confidential space in Southport town centre where you can talk openly, be heard and feel understood. I also provide online and telephone counselling across the UK.

I have worked extensively with people experiencing anxiety, overthinking, low mood and depression, low self-esteem, relationship difficulties and phobias, as well as difficulties linked to internet or social media use.

I also have experience supporting people in demanding professional roles who may appear to be coping well while feeling stressed, overwhelmed or under constant pressure. I have substantial experience working with autistic individuals, people with ADHD, and others who are neurodivergent.

How I work

Cognitive Behavioural Therapy

CBT explores how thoughts, feelings and behaviours are linked. By recognising unhelpful patterns, you can develop more effective ways of responding to difficult situations and triggers. CBT is collaborative, so we work together to identify practical ways forward.

Person-centred counselling

My person-centred approach provides a supportive, non-judgemental space where you can move at your own pace. I hold diplomas in both person-centred counselling and CBT, and draw on each according to what is most useful to you.

Mindfulness

I also draw on mindfulness where appropriate, helping you notice thoughts and feelings without becoming so caught up in them, and creating greater calm and perspective.

About me

I've never had counselling before. What should I expect?

A comfortable, confidential space where you can talk openly, be heard and feel understood. You set the pace — there is nothing you have to say and no script to follow. The first session is largely about getting to know what has brought you here.

If you need help now

In an emergency

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 999 or go to A&E.

If you need urgent mental health care but it is not immediately life-threatening, call NHS 111 and choose the option for urgent mental health support.
